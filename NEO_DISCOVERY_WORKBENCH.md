# NEO Discovery Workbench

Companion canonical compiler: [NEO Core Compiler](NEO_ANCHOR_COMPILER.md).

This file is the **exploratory mode** of NEO. It is used when the team does not yet know which representation, cancellation, Fourier geometry, tensor costume, observer or adversarial configuration is the right one to inspect. It is deliberately broader than the canonical compiler, but it does **not** enlarge the NEO ontology.

The fixed anchor interface remains
\[
\boxed{u(t),\qquad P,\qquad C=\operatorname{curl},\qquad C^2=(-\Delta)P,\qquad t.}
\]

Everything discovered here must eventually pass the same compile-back test:
\[
\boxed{\text{Can this phenomenon be reconstructed from }(u,P,C,C^2,t)\text{ by the NEO grammar?}}
\]
If yes, it is a costume, renderer, contraction, null set, audit witness or derived jet—not a new primitive.

The standing labels are **EXACT**, **DEDUCTION**, **INTERPRETATION**, **CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE**, **OPEN**, and **AUDIT**. Numerical experiments remain **AUDIT** only. No discovery in this file is a global regularity claim.

## 0. Which NEO file should I use? — DEDUCTION

### Use `NEO_ANCHOR_COMPILER.md` when

- the object or equation is already known and must be reconstructed from the anchors;
- the task is to identify canonical tensor/spectral parentage;
- the task is to normalize a first/second spectral jet;
- the task is to evolve a compiled renderer by the parabolic coproduct;
- the task is to prove that a proposed new quantity is only a costume;
- the team needs the smallest stable language for a proof or singularity normal form.

Its main advantage is **compression**:
\[
\boxed{\text{many NS faces}\longrightarrow\text{few compiler rules}.}
\]

### Use `NEO_DISCOVERY_WORKBENCH.md` when

- the relevant NS phenomenon is not yet known;
- one needs adversarial finite-Fourier examples or phase sweeps;
- a cancellation may be local, global, spectral, tensorial, Hodge or alignment-based and the cause is unclear;
- one needs to compare several readers of the same physical interaction;
- one needs to search for exact zero sets, degenerate strata, delayed appearances or hidden phase dependence;
- one needs to falsify a tempting mechanism before promoting any theorem to the core.

Its main advantage is **breadth**:
\[
\boxed{\text{one full state}\longrightarrow\text{many controlled views and stress tests}.}
\]

### Transition rule

Discovery mode is temporary. Once a phenomenon has exact parentage, return it to the Core:
\[
\boxed{
\text{observe}\to\text{stress-test}\to\text{compile back}\to\text{normalize}\to\text{promote or discard}.
}
\]

A discovery must **not** be promoted merely because it is visually compelling, numerically stable or has a memorable name.

## 1. Core versus Workbench — DEDUCTION

| Question | Core Compiler | Discovery Workbench |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal | reconstruction / normalization | exploration / falsification |
| Starting point | typed anchors and known target | full state and an unresolved phenomenon |
| Preferred objects | normal forms, exact parentage, type projectors | witnesses, readers, phases, special supports, adversarial families |
| Numerical role | audit signs/factors only | aggressive hypothesis testing |
| New names | strongly discouraged | allowed as temporary handles |
| Promotion criterion | exact compile-back and stable parentage | none by itself |
| Failure mode | over-compression before understanding | descendant/observer loop without closure |
| Exit condition | proof-ready normal form | return a distilled exact statement to Core |

The files are complementary, not competing. The Workbench may be larger and messier; the Core should become smaller as it becomes stronger.

## 2. Discovery grammar — DEDUCTION

The Workbench may freely vary **views**, not primitives. Useful views include:

1. **matrix face of curl:** directional derivatives, anisotropic readers compiled from entries of `C` and `C^2`;
2. **spectral face of curl:** `H`, `\Lambda`, helicity projectors and other fixed `f(C)` readers;
3. **Hodge routing:** `P/Q` decomposition of the same raw source;
4. **tensor routing:** spin-0/1/2 sectors from the isotropic tensor envelope;
5. **Fourier genealogy:** parent-child support, triads, quartets and repeated transport paths;
6. **contraction choice:** local field, global action, modal work, spectral moment or tensor pairing;
7. **jet order:** present value, time derivative, variational derivative or spectral derivative;
8. **adversarial strata:** Beltrami, pure helicity, one shell, collinear shear, near-degenerate roots, antipodal interactions and zero-curl seams.

These are observation coordinates on the same NEO-generated dynamics.

## 3. The main discipline: do not turn a view into a mechanism — DEDUCTION

A quantity may vanish because of very different reasons:

- the raw source is zero;
- `P` or `Q` removes it;
- a tensor projector removes it;
- an affine spectral reader cannot see it;
- a degenerate `C` eigenspace hides directional redistribution;
- a global integration-by-parts identity cancels it;
- phase alignment cancels a scalar contraction;
- the first nonzero contribution appears only at a later jet.

These causes must be distinguished during exploration, but they are not independent ontological species.

The Workbench therefore asks
\[
\boxed{
\text{What changed: source, routing, reader, contraction, support, or jet order?}
}
\]
before asking for a new mechanism.

## 4. Recommended adversarial battery — AUDIT

For a proposed structural claim, test at least the relevant subset of:

- monochromatic Beltrami pure helicity;
- mixed-radius same-helicity state;
- mixed-helicity single-shell state;
- active heterochiral triad;
- 2D-in-3D flow and a small 3D perturbation;
- same signed-curl eigenvalue on different Fourier directions;
- near-degenerate signed roots;
- high-high near-antipodal interaction with fixed low child;
- collinear shear;
- phase-translated spectrally isolated families;
- a state with overlapping genealogies into the same child;
- a zero-curl longitudinal pressure block distinct from zero Fourier frequency.

The purpose is not to accumulate examples. It is to find the smallest exact statement that survives all of them.

## 5. Reader discipline — DEDUCTION

Different readers can make the same interaction look qualitatively different. Therefore always separate
\[
\boxed{\text{physical source}}\qquad\text{from}\qquad\boxed{\text{observer response}.}
\]

Scalar spectral readers probe curvature in signed-curl space. Matrix/tensor readers can distinguish geometry inside a degenerate spectral eigenspace. Hodge and tensor projectors route components rather than create them. Global contractions may erase local activity.

A strong discovery is one whose dependence on the reader is itself explained by the Core compiler.

## 6. Jet discipline — DEDUCTION

A zero at the current state is not automatically a structural zero. When useful, prolong the **existing compiled expression** rather than inventing a descendant primitive.

Distinguish:

- physical-time prolongation under genuine NS;
- spectral/covariant differentiation of `f(C)`;
- variational/marked-slot differentiation.

The Core already provides separate algorithms for these axes. The Workbench uses them to diagnose when an apparent mechanism disappears or reappears.

## 7. Stop conditions — DEDUCTION

The Workbench must stop and return to the Core when any of the following occurs:

1. the phenomenon has an exact parentage in the current grammar;
2. multiple named mechanisms collapse to one normal form;
3. an apparent effect is shown to be only a reader/contraction artifact;
4. a proposed new primitive is reconstructed from the anchors;
5. further descendants merely move the unresolved freedom to the next jet without reducing an analytic gap.

The fifth condition is the anti-loop rule in exploratory form.

## 8. What belongs in `history/` — DEDUCTION

The archived files in `history/` remain valuable when one needs detailed genealogy of earlier approaches, control-volume constructions, traffic/material attempts, full-state frontiers or discarded closure routes.

Use them as:

- source material for counterexamples;
- provenance for exact identities already distilled into NEO;
- records of failed closure strategies;
- a warning against re-entering loops already exhausted.

Do not treat the archive as a second canonical ontology.

## 9. Promotion checklist — DEDUCTION

Before moving a Workbench result into `NEO_ANCHOR_COMPILER.md`, require all of:

1. **Type:** its carrier/tensor/spectral type is explicit.
2. **Parentage:** it compiles from `u,P,C,C^2,t` and allowed Euclidean operations.
3. **Exactness:** theorem and numerical evidence are clearly separated.
4. **Seams:** the relevant Beltrami/helicity/collinear/zero-frequency/degenerate cases have been attacked.
5. **No ontology growth:** no new independent state variable is required.
6. **Compression:** the statement replaces or normalizes several costumes rather than adding one more name.
7. **Analytic scope:** domain/localization limitations are stated.
8. **Proof value:** the result either improves reconstruction, eliminates a singular normal form, lowers an analytic gap, or supplies a genuinely useful rigidity statement.

If these fail, keep the result here as **AUDIT**, **INTERPRETATION**, **CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE** or **OPEN**.

## 10. Workbench motto — INTERPRETATION

The Core asks:
\[
\boxed{\text{How little language is sufficient to reconstruct this NS structure?}}
\]

The Workbench asks:
\[
\boxed{\text{How many hostile views can this proposed structure survive before we trust it?}}
\]

Together:
\[
\boxed{
\text{explore broadly}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{compile back}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{keep only the essence}.
}
\]
