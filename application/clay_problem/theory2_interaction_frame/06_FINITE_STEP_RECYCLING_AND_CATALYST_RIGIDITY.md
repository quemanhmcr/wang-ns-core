## 1. Causal-node frame and source/catalyst split — EXACT

\[

\tag{1.1}

\[
\Sigma(V)

Let

\[
P_\pm=\frac12(I\pm\operatorname{sgn}C),

W=P_+V,

z=P_-V.

Write

\[
N=\Gamma_VV,
\qquad
N_\pm=P_\pm N.

Define the pure positive-helicity source into the negative sheet

\boxed{
S=P_-\Gamma_WW.

\boxed{N_-=S+C_-.}

## 2. First-recycling identity — EXACT

Because `P_-` is fixed in the causal-node frame and `Gamma` is bilinear,

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_\tau
&=P_-
\left(
\Gamma_{W_\tau}W+
\Gamma_WW_\tau
\right)\\
&=
-\,P_-
\left(
\Gamma_{N_+}W+
\Gamma_WN_+
\right)\\
&\quad
-\nu P_-
\left(
\Gamma_{\Lambda^2W}W+
\Gamma_W\Lambda^2W
\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
S_\tau
=-P_-
(\Gamma_{N_+}W+\Gamma_WN_+)
-\nu P_-
(\Gamma_{\Lambda^2W}W+\Gamma_W\Lambda^2W).
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The minus sign in the first term is essential. It comes from

\[
W_\tau=-N_+-\nu\Lambda^2W.
\]

Now define only as a scalar diagnostic

\[
\boxed{
\Xi(\tau)=-\operatorname{Re}\langle S,z\rangle.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Using (1.6) and (2.1), together with self-adjointness of `Lambda^2`, gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Xi_\tau
={}&
\|S\|_2^2
+\operatorname{Re}\langle S,C_-\rangle\\
&+
\operatorname{Re}
\left\langle
P_-(\Gamma_{N_+}W+\Gamma_WN_+),
 z
\right\rangle\\
&+
\nu\operatorname{Re}
\left\langle
P_-(\Gamma_{\Lambda^2W}W+\Gamma_W\Lambda^2W)
+\Lambda^2S,
 z
\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

This identity does **not** say that `||S||_2^2` is irreversible dissipation. Every positive term can still be canceled. Its content is narrower:

\[
\boxed{
\text{persistent cancellation of the actual source }S
\text{ must pay for }\|S\|_2^2
\text{ through correlations with the actual catalyst }z.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

The largest renewal coefficient is itself generated by the current state,

\[
N_+=P_+\Gamma_VV.
\]

This closure requirement is absent from an externally prescribed mixer.

### Localized form

For a limiting open core with an infinite relative-IR wake, global `L^2` pairings need not be the right topology. Let `Q` be a smooth work-output window and `Q^sharp` a fixed enlarged window. Applying the same calculation to `QS` and `Qz` produces commutator/cutoff terms. The intended use is

\[
\text{localized identity}
=
\text{(2.3) on }Q
+
\operatorname{Err}_{\rm IR/UV}(Q,Q^\sharp).
\]

The three-scale estimates and the historical UV edge are supposed to make these errors arbitrarily small after the fattening width is fixed sufficiently large. This localization step is `OPEN` at the exact quantitative level used later.

## 3. Exact linear-in-catalyst critical work

Use the convention of `00`:

\[
\mathcal T(V)
=-\operatorname{Re}\langle \Gamma_VV,\Lambda V\rangle.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a pure helicity field, critical norm equals signed helicity up to sign; Euler helicity conservation therefore gives

\[
\mathcal T(W)=\mathcal T(z)=0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Hence the cubic expansion contains only the `W^2z` and `Wz^2` sectors.

Let `T_{2,1}(W,z)` denote the part linear in `z`. Polarize the exact Euler helicity identity

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle \Gamma_VV,CV\rangle=0
\]

at `V=W+epsilon z`. Since

\[
CW=\Lambda W,
\qquad
Cz=-\Lambda z,
\]

the coefficient of `epsilon` gives

\[
\operatorname{Re}
\langle
\Gamma_Wz+\Gamma_zW,
\Lambda W
\rangle
=
\operatorname{Re}\langle S,\Lambda z\rangle.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Substitution into the critical cubic expansion yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_{2,1}(W,z)
=-2\operatorname{Re}\langle S,\Lambda z\rangle
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

with the sign reversed if the global convention for `T` is reversed.

The remaining sector contains two copies of `z`. On a fixed normalized fat window all relevant Sobolev/Bernstein norms are equivalent, so if

\[
A\sim\|Q^\sharp W\|,
\qquad
\delta\sim\|Q^\sharp z\|,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal T_{1,2}|
\lesssim A\delta^2
+\operatorname{Err}_{\rm loc}.
}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal T_Q(V)|
\lesssim
\|QS\|_2\,\delta
+A\delta^2
+\operatorname{Err}_{\rm loc}.
}
\tag{3.6}
\]

No comparable-helicity-mass conclusion has been used.

## 4. One-step source control on a recurrent work window — DEDUCTION

Assume on a fixed normalized interval or compact recurrent component:

1. `Q subset Q^sharp` are fixed smooth comparable-frequency windows;
2. `A` bounds the positive-helicity work-active component in `Q^sharp`;
3. `delta` bounds the overlapping negative-helicity component in `Q^sharp`;
4. `delta << A` in the homochiral-dominant branch;
5. remote IR/UV contributions are represented by `o_L(1)` after a sufficiently fat window is chosen.

Fixed-band bilinear estimates give schematically

\[
\boxed{
\|QC_-\|_2
\lesssim
A\delta+\delta^2+o_L(1),
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\|Q^\sharp N_+\|_2
\lesssim
A^2+A\delta+\delta^2+o_L(1),
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\|Q P_-(\Gamma_{N_+}W+\Gamma_WN_+)\|_2
\lesssim
A^3+A^2\delta+A\delta^2+o_L(1).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Average the localized version of (2.3) over a recurrent orbit, or over a sequence of recurrent node intervals for which the normalized `Xi` boundary contribution tends to zero. Young's inequality absorbs the terms linear in `QS`. In the regime `A >= c nu` and `delta << A` one obtains the schematic source estimate

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\|QS\|_2^2}
\lesssim
A^3\delta
+\nu A^2\delta
+A^2\delta^2
+o_L(1).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The exact constants depend only on the fixed windows and the compact work-active class.

This is an **average dynamic cancellation estimate**, not a source-norm monotonicity formula.

## 5. First catalyst lower bounds — DEDUCTION from (3.6) and (4.4)

Assume the normalized work block is genuinely productive:

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\mathcal T_Q}
\ge c_0\nu A^2.
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are two cases.

### Case A — the quadratic-catalyst sector carries a fixed fraction

If

\[
A\delta^2
\gtrsim\nu A^2,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\delta^2\gtrsim\nu A.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the square-root catalyst scale.

### Case B — the linear-in-catalyst sector carries a fixed fraction

Then, by (3.6), Cauchy--Schwarz in time, and (4.4),

\[
\nu A^2
\lesssim
\delta\,
\overline{\|QS\|_2^2}^{1/2}.
\]

In the genuinely homochiral-dominant range the leading term in (4.4) gives

\[
\nu A^2
\lesssim
A^{3/2}\delta^{3/2},
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\delta^3\gtrsim\nu^2A,
\qquad
\delta\gtrsim\nu^{2/3}A^{1/3}.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

This already excludes the sharp catalytic asymptotic

\[
\boxed{
A/\nu\to\infty,
\qquad
\delta/\nu=O(1),
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

provided the recurrent localized source estimate (4.4) is available uniformly.

The weakness of (5.3) is quantitative: it used the absolute renewal bound `O(A^3)`. A tiny residual source may have a very large first derivative because large broadband contributions cancel at order zero but not at order one. The next sections address exactly that temporal-superoscillation loophole.

## 6. Gaussian UV edge as a sufficient historical input

The historical leading-edge target in `02` is super-geometric. If that stronger barrier is proved, it implies in particular a weaker Gaussian-in-shell envelope. For the recycling argument it is enough to assume

\[
\boxed{
a_q(\tau)
\lesssim
\begin{cases}
\sqrt M,&q\le0,\\
C\exp[-c(q-D)^2],&q>0,
\end{cases}
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

uniformly on the normalized causal interval.

Thus the recycling theorem needs only a Gaussian corollary of the historical UV edge; it does not require the full strength of a double-exponential shell barrier.

For each integer `m>=0`, Bernstein gives

\[
\|\nabla^mP_qV\|_\infty
\lesssim
2^{(m+1)q}a_q.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For `q<=0` the sum is geometrically convergent. For `q>0`, maximizing

\[
(m+1)(\log2)q-c(q-D)^2
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\nabla^mV\|_\infty
\le C_0\exp(C_1m^2).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

Likewise, for `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
\|\nabla^mV\|_2
\le C_0\exp(C_1m^2)
}
\tag{6.4}
\]

on the normalized node class, with the understanding that the global `m=0` `L^2` norm need not remain uniformly tight in a limiting infinite-IR wake.

The crucial point is that the IR wake becomes easier under positive spatial derivatives. The temporal-complexity obstruction comes from UV, precisely where (6.1) is strongest.

## 7. Complete recycling-jet lemma — OPEN prove-next theorem

Repeatedly differentiate

\[
V_\tau=-\Gamma_VV-\nu\Lambda^2V.
\]

The `r`-th time derivative is a finite differential polynomial in `V`; its polynomial degree is at most `r+1`, and no term contains more than `2r` spatial derivatives. The number of Leibniz terms grows at most factorially.

Because

\[
r!C^r\ll\exp(C'r^2),
\]

the Gaussian spatial bounds (6.3)--(6.4), together with fixed-window paraproduct/locality estimates, suggest the induction

\[
\boxed{
\|Q\partial_\tau^rV\|_2
\le
C_0A_0\Omega^r\exp(C_1r^2),
\qquad r\ge0,
}
\tag{7.1}
\]

where

\[
\Omega\lesssim_{M,Q,Q^\sharp}1+\nu+A_0
\]

and `A_0` is a uniform complete finite-window size on the causal class.

### Why this is a complete-state jet

The maps

\[
V\mapsto E_V=[\Gamma_V,C],
\qquad
V\mapsto A_a(V)=[\Gamma_V,H_a]
\]

are linear in `V`, and

\[
\mathscr O_a(V)
=H_aA_a(V)-A_a(H_aV)
\]

is linear as well. Therefore any fixed-window operator norm continuous under these maps inherits the same time-jet growth from (7.1). The desired estimate is thus a **complete Theory-2 recycling-jet bound**, not a scalar shell hierarchy.

### What must actually be proved

A rigorous proof of (7.1) has to control:

- the combinatorics of repeated time differentiation;
- fat-window commutators;
- low--high paraproducts from the noncompact IR wake;
- the high-frequency tail through (6.1);
- and constants uniformly over the compact causal-node class.

No inversion of the full flag family is involved.

## 8. Finite-order temporal interpolation — DEDUCTION from (7.1)

Assume the overlapping negative profile stays small on a fixed normalized interval `I`:

\[
\boxed{
\|Q^\sharp z\|_{L^\infty(I;L^2)}\le\delta.
}
\tag{8.1}
\]

Work on a fixed interior subinterval `I' compactly contained in I`; endpoint corrections are then absorbed into the constants. The Banach/Hilbert-valued Landau--Kolmogorov interpolation inequality gives, for each integer `r>=2`,

\[
\|Qz_\tau\|_{L^\infty(I';L^2)}
\lesssim
C_r
\delta^{1-1/r}
\left(
A_0\Omega^re^{Cr^2}
\right)^{1/r}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

For the standard finite-difference proof one may take `log C_r=O(log r)` at the level relevant here. Put

\[
R=\frac{eA_0}{\delta}.
\]

Choosing

\[
r\asymp\sqrt{\log R}
\]

gives the finite-step estimate

\[
\boxed{
\|Qz_\tau\|_{L^\infty(I';L^2)}
\lesssim
\Omega\delta
\exp\left[
C\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{eA_0}{\delta}\right)}
\right].
}
\tag{8.3}
\]

For every fixed `A_0/delta`, only finitely many recycling derivatives are used:

\[
\boxed{
r\sim\sqrt{\log(A_0/\delta)}.}
\tag{8.4}
\]

This is the central anti-repacking feature. The theorem is finite-order; it does not classify an infinite cancellation kernel.

## 9. Pointwise source bound and near-square-root catalyst law

Project the negative equation (1.6) onto the work-output window:

\tag{9.1}

Finite-window locality gives

\[

\|QC_-\|_2
\lesssim
(1+\nu+A_0)\delta+o_L(1).
\tag{9.2}
\]

Combining (8.3) and (9.2),

\[
\boxed{
\|QS\|_2
\lesssim
\Omega\delta
\exp\left[
C\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{eA_0}{\delta}\right)}
\right]
+o_L(1).
}
\tag{9.3}
\]

Insert this into the exact work decomposition (3.6). A persistently productive normalized block satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_Q
\ge c_0\nu A_0^2
}
\tag{9.4}
\]

and `A_0` comparable to the work-active mixer size must obey

\[
\boxed{
\nu A_0^2
\lesssim
\Omega\delta^2
\exp\left[
C\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{eA_0}{\delta}\right)}
\right]
+o_L(1).
}
\tag{9.5}
\]

In the large-mixer regime

\[
A_0\gg1+\nu,
\qquad
\Omega\simeq A_0,
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
\delta
\gtrsim
\sqrt{\nu A_0}
\exp\left[
-C\sqrt{\log\left(\frac{eA_0}{\delta}\right)}
\right].
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

The exponential factor is subpower in `A_0/delta`. Therefore, if

\[
\delta\le C_*\nu,

and `R=A_0/nu`, (9.5) implies

\[
R
\lesssim
C\exp(C\sqrt{\log(eR)}).
\tag{9.7}
\]

Since

\[
\frac{R}{\exp(C\sqrt{\log R})}\to\infty,
\]

`R` is bounded by a constant depending only on the compact class and `C_*`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\delta=O(\nu)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A_0/\nu=O(1)
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

under the hypotheses of Sections 6--9.

This is the precise worst-enemy exclusion supplied by the finite-step route. It does **not** assert

\[
\|V_-\|\simeq\|V_+\|.

## 10. Why the standard falsifiers do not invalidate the theorem target

### 10.1 Externally prescribed fast mixer

An arbitrary mixer can prescribe temporal frequency

\[
\Omega_{\rm ext}\gg A+\nu
\]

independently of its spatial amplitude. A source of size `A^2` can then drive a response of size

\[
\delta\sim A^2/\Omega_{\rm ext},
\]

and choosing `Omega_ext ~ A^2/nu` makes `delta ~ nu` possible.

Such a mixer is not constrained by (7.1). In NS, every time derivative is recursively generated by the same quadratic convolution law and inherits the historical spatial-frequency envelope. This is exactly where self-generation enters the argument.

### 10.2 Beltrami/shear/null state

If the positive-helicity component is nonlinear-null at leading order, then

\[
S=0.
\]

No false source penalty is produced. Critical work begins at higher order in the perturbing catalyst, consistent with the previously identified square-root-type scaling.

### 10.3 Broadband angular cancellation

`S` is the **actual summed vector source** after all same-output phase cancellations. The theorem never counts individual triads. Occupying every side mode therefore does not evade the estimate.

### 10.4 Infinite IR wake

For `q<<0`, the factors

\[
2^{(m+1)q}a_q
\]

are geometrically summable. An IR wake may carry infinite critical mass while remaining harmless for positive spatial derivative bounds. The dangerous fast-time mechanism would have to come from UV, where the historical edge acts.

### 10.5 Finite-mode truncations

A finite Waleffe/Galerkin system respecting the true quadratic coefficient bounds satisfies a finite jet bound at least as strong as (7.1). A truncation is a false counterexample only if it deletes the reality/Toeplitz side channels of the full convolution algebra.

## 12. Remaining technical seam

Three points remain genuinely open.

1. **Historical edge.** Prove the moving-record leading-edge theorem in `02`, or at least its Gaussian corollary (6.1), with constants uniform on the causal class.
2. **Work-local lift.** Prove a dynamic complete profile theorem ensuring that the `delta` in (8.1) is the negative-helicity profile actually overlapping the productive work block; spatially separated helicity mass must decouple.
3. **Complete recycling jet.** Prove (7.1) with smooth fat windows and all IR/UV commutator errors controlled uniformly.

Once these are available, the finite-step interpolation and the exclusion (9.8) are ordinary deductions.

The surviving enemy after (9.8) is therefore narrower than the general broadband mixer of `05`: it cannot use an `O(nu)` catalyst to service an arbitrarily larger homochiral work-active mixer by hidden temporal superoscillation. Any remaining recurrent mixer must either carry a larger overlapping catalyst, leave the homochiral-dominant regime, or escape one of the three hypotheses above.
