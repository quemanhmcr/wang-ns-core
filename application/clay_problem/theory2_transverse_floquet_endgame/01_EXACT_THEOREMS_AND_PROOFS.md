## Theorem 1 — Poisson Formation identities

For

\[
J_u b=P(b\times Cu),\qquad N(u)=J_u u,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{J_u^*=-J_u,\qquad J_u(Cu)=0.}
\]

Define

\[
K_u=[C,J_u].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{K_u b=-2P(S(Cu)b),\qquad K_u^*=K_u,\qquad CN(u)=K_u u.}
\]

### Proof

Skewness follows from the scalar triple product and self-adjointness of \(P\) on divergence-free fields. The identity \(J_u(Cu)=P(Cu\times Cu)=0\). Since \(C^*=C\) and \(J_u^*=-J_u\),

\[
[C,J_u]^*=[C,J_u].
\]

Finally,

\[
K_u u=CJ_u u-J_uCu=CN(u).
\]

The explicit strain formula follows by expanding the curl commutator. ∎

## Theorem 2 — Critical work identities

Let \(\Lambda=|C|=HC\). Then

\[
\boxed{
W
=2\langle \Lambda u,N(u)\rangle
=\langle u,[\Lambda,J_u]u\rangle
=2\langle Hu,K_u u\rangle.
}
\]

### Proof

Using \(J_u^*=-J_u\),

\[
\langle u,[\Lambda,J_u]u\rangle
=\langle \Lambda u,J_u u\rangle-\langle J_u u,\Lambda u\rangle
=2\langle \Lambda u,N\rangle.
\]

Because \(\Lambda=HC\) and \(CN=K_u u\),

\[
\langle \Lambda u,N\rangle=\langle Hu,CN\rangle=\langle Hu,K_u u\rangle.
\]

∎

## Theorem 3 — Constrained-gradient decomposition

Let

\[
G=\Lambda(\Lambda-a-bC)u
\]

with \(a,b\) chosen so that

\[
\langle u,G\rangle=\langle Cu,G\rangle=0.
\]

For \(d^2=\|G\|_{H^{-1/2}}^2>0\), define

\[
\gamma=\frac{W}{2d^2}.
\]

Then there is a unique decomposition

\[
\boxed{N=\gamma G+T}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\langle T,u\rangle=
\langle T,Cu\rangle=
\langle T,\Lambda u\rangle=0.
}
\]

Moreover

\[
|b|\le1,\qquad a\ge0.
\]

### Proof

The vector \(G\) is the \(H^{-1/2}\)-gradient of \(M\) projected tangent to the joint energy-helicity leaf. Since

\[
(N,G)_{H^{-1/2}}=\langle \Lambda u,N\rangle=W/2,
\]

the orthogonal projection coefficient of \(N\) onto \(G\) is \(W/(2d^2)=\gamma\). The remainder is therefore orthogonal to \(G\) and to the two leaf normals, giving the three displayed pairings. The regression inequalities for \(a,b\) are the Cauchy bounds of the two-constraint projection. ∎

## Theorem 4 — Heat remainder and strict critical gap

Define

\[
R^{\rm heat}:=a\Lambda u+bC\Lambda u.
\]

Then

\[
\Lambda^2u=G+R^{\rm heat},
\]

and the two summands are orthogonal in \(H^{-1/2}\). Hence

\[
\boxed{D_3=d^2+\|R^{\rm heat}\|_{H^{-1/2}}^2.}
\]

Furthermore

\[
\boxed{D_3-d^2\ge \frac{D_2^2}{M}>0}
\]

for every nonzero nondegenerate state.

### Proof

The first identity is the definition of \(G\). Orthogonality is exactly the projection condition. The quantitative lower bound is the Cauchy inequality for the residual heat component against \(\Lambda u\). ∎

## Corollary 5 — Critical balance

For Navier–Stokes,

\[
M'=W-2\nu D_3=2\gamma d^2-2\nu D_3.
\]

Thus instantaneous critical neutrality implies

\[
\boxed{\gamma=\nu\frac{D_3}{d^2}>\nu.}
\]

For a neutral time cell,

\[
\boxed{
\int(\gamma-\nu)d^2
=\nu\int(D_3-d^2)
\ge\nu\int\frac{D_2^2}{M}>0.
}
\]

This is an exact scalar constraint; \(T\) performs no direct critical work.

## Theorem 6 — Polarized Curl–Killing and physical companions

For arbitrary divergence-free \(a,b\),

\[
\boxed{J_a(Cb)+J_b(Ca)=0.}
\]

If \(Ca=xa\) and \(Cb=yb\), then

\[
\boxed{2B(a_x,b_y)=(x-y)P(b_y\times a_x).}
\]

Hence equal signed curl roots do not interact. For a real unequal-root pair at frequencies \(p,m\), reality supplies both outputs

\[
p+m,\qquad p-m.
\]

Their squared-radius defects satisfy

\[
\boxed{
(|p+m|^2-|p|^2)+(|p-m|^2-|p|^2)=2|m|^2.
}
\]

### Proof

Polarize the identity \(J_u(Cu)=0\) in \(u=a+b\). Substitution of the curl eigenvalue relations gives the second formula. The companion identity is elementary expansion of the two squared norms. ∎

## Theorem 7 — Actual-state rank-one completion

For a common-state incidence rectangle, the coefficient matrix factors as

\[
Z_{ij}=A_iB_j.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{Z_{ii}Z_{jj}=Z_{ij}Z_{ji}.}
\]

More generally, every Laurent-monomial identity valid for arbitrary nonzero factors \(A_i,B_j\) is generated by alternating cycles of the finite bipartite incidence graph.

### Proof

For exponents \(n_{ij}\),

\[
\prod Z_{ij}^{n_{ij}}
=
\prod_i A_i^{\sum_jn_{ij}}
\prod_j B_j^{\sum_in_{ij}}.
\]

This equals \(1\) for all nonzero factors iff every row and column exponent sum vanishes. Those integer zero-boundary edge vectors are precisely the cycle lattice of the bipartite graph. ∎

## Theorem 8 — Finite completed-network saturation rigidity

Consider a **finite**, reality-complete physical interaction network satisfying:

1. all rank-one cross incidences forced by same-output cancellation are retained;
2. active unequal-shell interactions are separated from same-root, collinear, Leray-null and equal-radius exceptional strata;
3. the established finite-support unequal-shell completion lemma applies: a finite exact unequal-shell completion with no exported physical output is globally collinear/null.

Then

\[
\boxed{T=\lambda R_{\rm fv},\qquad \lambda>0}
\]

is impossible on the network.

### Proof

Both \(G\) and \(R_{\rm fv}\) are supported on occupied state packets. If \(T=\lambda R_{\rm fv}\), then

\[
N=\gamma G+\lambda R_{\rm fv}
\]

has no output outside the occupied packet family. Hence every physical Formation output outside the occupied support must cancel exactly.

Same-output cancellation preserves the actual-state rank-one factorization, so Theorem 7 forces the corresponding cross incidences. Reality forces the companion outputs as well. Because the network is finite, repeated physical completion cannot export indefinitely. Therefore exact closure with no new occupied output is reached in finitely many steps.

By the finite-support unequal-shell completion lemma, such a finite exact nonexporting completion is necessarily collinear/null (or another explicitly excluded exceptional geometry), contradicting hypothesis 2. ∎

### Scope

This theorem is only finite-network rigidity. It does **not** imply that an infinite PDE state contains a finite exact completed witness. That missing extraction step is OPEN and is isolated in `04_OPEN_FRONTIER.md`.
