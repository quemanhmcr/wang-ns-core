# Curl Spectral Reduction of Formation Geometry

This note records the strongest geometric compression produced by the deep formation–signature campaign.  The key point is that the third core is not merely an equivalence between two coordinate systems.  Curl supplies a distinguished spectral reduction of the formation connection.

The notation is the same as in the parent cores:

\[
\mathcal C_{NS}
=(\mathfrak g_\sigma,g_{L^2},T,C),
\qquad C=\operatorname{curl},
\]

with Levi–Civita connection \(\nabla\) generated from \((g,T)\).

## 1. Mother as a spectral soldering-type form

The complete mother is

\[
\boxed{
\Theta:=E=d_\nabla C,
\qquad
\Theta(u)=E_u=[\nabla_u,C].
}
\]

The map \(u\mapsto E_u\) is linear in the state slot.  On the canonical periodic mean-zero class, the existing spectral-signature theorem makes this map injective.  In the finite physical coordinate audit its rank was \(28/28\), with condition number about \(2.45\); after adding constant Galilean modes, the nullity became exactly three.

Thus \(E\) should be read as an operator-valued **soldering-type one-form**: it attaches each physical state direction to a distinguished operator tangent direction generated by curl.  This does not mean that the signature image is itself a curved embedded manifold; see Section 8 below.

## 2. Curl isospectral orbit and stabilizer

Let \(C\) be represented in an orthonormal spectral frame.  For any skew connection matrix \(A\),

\[
C(t)=e^{tA}Ce^{-tA}
\]

stays on the orthogonal isospectral orbit of \(C\), and

\[
\boxed{
\dot C(0)=[A,C].
}
\]

Taking \(A=\nabla_u\) gives

\[
\boxed{
E_u=[\nabla_u,C]
}
\]

as the orbit velocity of curl induced by the formation connection.

The orbit stabilizer is the skew commutant

\[
\mathfrak h_C
=\{A:A^*=-A,\ [A,C]=0\}.
\]

Its orthogonal complement in the skew operator space will be denoted \(\mathfrak m_C\).  Therefore the connection splits

\[
\boxed{
\nabla=V+B,
\qquad
V\in\mathfrak h_C,
\quad
B\in\mathfrak m_C.
}
\]

Here:

- \(V\) rotates vectors **inside one curl eigenspace**;
- \(B\) mixes **different curl eigenspaces**.

Since \([V,C]=0\),

\[
\boxed{
E=[B,C].
}
\]

On a spectral slot \(i\to j\),

\[
E_{ij}=(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)B_{ij},
\]

hence off the degenerate diagonal

\[
\boxed{
B_{ij}=\frac{E_{ij}}{\lambda_j-\lambda_i}.
}
\]

This is the geometric meaning of the divided-difference and spectral-gap formulas that repeatedly appeared in the spectral-signature core.

## 3. Physical state space is a small distribution inside the orbit tangent

In the 28-dimensional physical Fourier coordinate lab, curl had multiplicities

\[
2,6,6,6,6,2.
\]

The full skew connection space had dimension \(378\), the curl stabilizer had dimension \(62\), and the orthogonal isospectral orbit tangent therefore had dimension \(316\).  The physical mother image had only dimension \(28\):

\[
\boxed{
E(\mathfrak g_\sigma)
\subset
T_C\mathcal O_C,
\qquad
\dim E(\mathfrak g_\sigma)=28\ll316.
}
\]

Thus the NS state is not an arbitrary tangent direction of the curl orbit.  The formation tensor selects a very special low-dimensional distribution inside that ambient spectral geometry.

Finite-difference conjugation experiments recovered \(E_u\) as \(\dot C(0)\), while all tested spectral invariants had zero first variation at roundoff.  This is the precise sense in which the state is encoded by **how the curl spectral frame turns**, not by changing the eigenvalues of curl.

## 4. Curvature split: Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci form

Let

\[
R(a,b)
=[\nabla_a,\nabla_b]-\nabla_{[a,b]}
\]

be formation curvature.  With the spectral splitting \(\nabla=V+B\), decompose

\[
R=R_\parallel+R_\perp,
\]

where \(R_\parallel\) commutes with \(C\) and \(R_\perp\) changes curl spectral sheets.

In the constant-base spectral algebra, the block equations reduce to the familiar Gauss–Codazzi–Ricci pattern:

\[
\boxed{
R_\parallel
=[V,V]+\Pi_\parallel[B,B],
}
\]

while \(R_\perp\) is the Codazzi-type cross-sheet combination containing the mixed \(V\)-\(B\) terms and, when there are at least three spectral blocks, the off-sheet component of \([B,B]\).

The curvature mother is

\[
\boxed{
K:=d_\nabla E=d_\nabla^2C=[R,C].
}
\]

Because \([R_\parallel,C]=0\),

\[
\boxed{
K=[R_\perp,C].
}
\]

Thus \(K\) is the gap-weighted **Codazzi/off-sheet curvature sensor**.  The vertical Gauss/Ricci part is invisible to this single commutator.

The finite spectral tribunal verified the block identities at machine precision.  A separate full pseudospectral helical implementation verified the same decomposition on physical divergence-free fields, with Gauss residuals near \(10^{-15}\), Codazzi residuals near \(3\times10^{-15}\), and off-sheet curvature recovery from \(K\) around \(10^{-13}\).

## 5. Two distinct vertical curvature species

The decomposition reveals two conceptually different parts of \(R_\parallel\).

### 5.1. Extrinsic / sheet-mixing Gauss curvature

Even when \(V=0\), the horizontal sheet-mixing field \(B\) can produce

\[
\Pi_\parallel[B,B]\neq0.
\]

This vertical curvature is not genuinely hidden, because \(B\) is already reconstructed from \(E\).  In the spectral lab, pure-\(B\) data produced nonzero vertical curvature that was reconstructed from the known mother to roundoff.

### 5.2. Intrinsic stabilizer curvature

If \(B=0\) and the stabilizer block is nonabelian, then

\[
[V,V]\neq0,
\]

while

\[
E=0,
\qquad
K=0.
\]

For a \(3+3\) block decomposition, a pure vertical \(so(3)\oplus so(3)\) connection produced order-one intrinsic curvature while remaining completely invisible to the first two curl sensors.

This is the cleanest abstract model of a genuinely dark spectral-sheet curvature sector.

## 6. Reductive splitting and why two sheets are special

The stabilizer splitting satisfies

\[
[\mathfrak h_C,\mathfrak h_C]\subset\mathfrak h_C,
\qquad
[\mathfrak h_C,\mathfrak m_C]\subset\mathfrak m_C.
\]

So it is always a reductive decomposition.

For exactly two spectral blocks, one also has

\[
\boxed{
[\mathfrak m_C,\mathfrak m_C]\subset\mathfrak h_C.
}
\]

This is a symmetric-space type situation.  Pure sheet mixing can then generate vertical Gauss curvature without generating off-sheet curvature, so one may have

\[
E\neq0,
\qquad
R_\parallel\neq0,
\qquad
K=0.
\]

For three or more spectral blocks, two-hop mixing \(i\to j\to k\) makes \([\mathfrak m_C,\mathfrak m_C]\) acquire an \(\mathfrak m_C\)-component.  Random three- and four-block tests found substantial horizontal fractions, explaining why pure-\(B\) Codazzi curvature reappears in multi-sheet geometry.

## 7. Cartan/Bianchi form of the tower

On the endomorphism bundle induced by the formation connection,

\[
\boxed{
\Theta=d_\nabla C,
\qquad
K=d_\nabla\Theta=[R,C].
}
\]

The standard covariant identities then give

\[
\boxed{
 d_\nabla K=R\wedge\Theta,
\qquad
 d_\nabla R=0,
}
\]

and the next tested level

\[
\boxed{
 d_\nabla^2K=R\wedge K.
}
\]

A full physical pseudospectral tribunal verified Jacobi, \(d_\nabla\Theta=[R,C]\), the second Bianchi identity, and the next two levels with residuals between \(10^{-15}\) and \(10^{-13}\).

These identities are instances of standard connection/curvature geometry.  What is specific to this NS programme is not the existence of Bianchi identities; it is the distinguished physical choice \(C=\operatorname{curl}\), the completeness of \(d_\nabla C\) as a state sensor, and the spectral tomography supplied by the shifted flags.

## 8. Curved representation does not mean curved embedding

The signature map is linear on the fixed periodic state space.  After transporting the \(L^2\) metric, its image is a linear subspace with a constant metric.  As an ordinary embedded vector space it is flat.

Nevertheless, the formation Levi–Civita connection and its curvature transport to that image.  An exact \(so(3)\) audit produced nonzero formation curvature, reconstructed it on the signature side to roundoff, while the naive constant-coordinate curvature of the linear image remained exactly zero.

Therefore the phrase **curved representation** means

\[
\boxed{
\text{a representation carrying the curved formation connection},
}
\]

not

\[
\boxed{
\text{a geometrically curved embedding of the signature image itself}.
}
\]

This distinction should be preserved in all future statements.

## 9. Higher Bianchi degrees and vertical observability

Although \(K=[R,C]\) deletes the curl-commuting part of \(R\) at degree two, higher covariant degrees can couple that hidden curvature back to already-visible sensors:

\[
 d_\nabla K=R\wedge E,
\qquad
 d_\nabla^2K=R\wedge K.
\]

On full physical helical decompositions, the vertical curvature contribution to \(d_\nabla K\) was about \(24\%-29\%\) of the tested norm, with median \(27\%\).  At degree four the corresponding contribution remained about \(20\%\).

In abstract curvature-identification problems, this produces a genuine observability filtration.  For example, a \(3+3\) spectrum left one vertical curvature mode unresolved after degree three, while degree four removed the last null direction.  A fully vertical sector with \(E=K=0\) remained dark at every tested degree.

However, when curvature is constrained to come from an actual compatible connection, generic \(E+K\) already reconstructed the hidden vertical connection in all tested degeneracy patterns.  Higher degrees then act primarily as compatibility/Bianchi redundancy rather than automatically adding new independent connection data.

This distinction is essential:

\[
\boxed{
\text{curvature-as-independent-data filtration}
\neq
\text{connection-constrained inverse problem}.
}
\]

## 10. Tangent first, stabilizer later

The first two sensors satisfy

\[
E\in\mathfrak m_C,
\qquad
K\in\mathfrak m_C,
\]

so they are tangent to the curl isospectral orbit.

Higher Bianchi levels need not remain tangent.  In generic finite spectral tests, the stabilizer/normal component dominated \(d_\nabla K\) and \(d_\nabla^2K\), often contributing roughly \(80\%-97\%\) of the measured norm.  This is the algebraic mechanism by which information about within-sheet curvature re-enters after the first orbit-tangent levels.

A fully vertical connection remains a strict dark control: \(E=K=dK=d^2K=0\) even though intrinsic stabilizer curvature may be nonzero.

## 11. Curvature functional calculus also has one mother

For any sufficiently regular scalar reader \(f\), spectral divided differences give

\[
\boxed{
[R,f(C)]_{xy}
=f^{[1]}(x,y)[R,C]_{xy}.
}
\]

Polynomial, exponential, trigonometric, absolute-value and hinge readers were tested on spectra with degeneracies, with worst residual below \(6\times10^{-16}\).

Thus \(K=[R,C]\) is the **curvature mother** for the first curl functional calculus, exactly as \(E=[\nabla,C]\) is the mother for first connection deformation.

Again, this does not recover curvature that commutes with \(C\); it only says that changing the scalar spectral reader does not create a new cross-sheet curvature species.

## 12. Canonical geometric compression

The deepest current compression is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{formation geometry:}&\quad (g,T)\Rightarrow\nabla,R,\\
\text{curl spectral reduction:}&\quad \nabla=V+B,\\
\text{degree one:}&\quad E=[B,C],\\
\text{degree two:}&\quad K=[R_\perp,C],\\

\text{Bianchi coupling:}&\quad d_\nabla K=R\wedge E,\quad d_\nabla R=0.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

In words:

> Curl decomposes formation geometry into spectral sheets.  The complete mother measures how the formation connection mixes those sheets.  Curvature splits into within-sheet Gauss/Ricci and cross-sheet Codazzi parts.  The curvature mother sees the cross-sheet part, while higher Bianchi couplings can expose how the hidden within-sheet geometry acts on already-visible spectral deformation.

This is the current canonical geometric interpretation of the third core.
