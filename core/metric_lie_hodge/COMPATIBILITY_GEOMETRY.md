# Compatibility Geometry

This note records the principal descendants of the formation datum.  They are not additional primitives of the equation.

## 1. Material and Poisson curl mothers

Two independently useful curl incompatibility operators are

\[
E_u=[\nabla_u,C]
\]

and

\[
K_u=[C,\mathcal J_u].
\]

Let the deformation/shape operator of the state be

\[
S_u^{\mathrm{def}}(b)=\nabla_bu.
\]

Metric compatibility gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_u=(S_u^{\mathrm{def}})^*-S_u^{\mathrm{def}}.
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
[C,\mathcal J_u]
=2\,\operatorname{sym}[S_u^{\mathrm{def}},C].
}
\]

Using torsion freeness,

\[
S_u^{\mathrm{def}}=\nabla_u-\operatorname{ad}_u,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
[C,\mathcal J_u]
=2\,\operatorname{sym}\bigl([\nabla_u,C]-[\operatorname{ad}_u,C]\bigr).
}
\]

Thus the material and Hamiltonian curl mothers are slot/adjoint renderings of the same metric-Lie/curl geometry rather than independent mechanisms.

## 2. Spectral readers are functorial coordinates

For a typed functional calculus reader \(f(C)\), the material commutator obeys

\[
\boxed{
[\nabla_u,f(C)]=Df_C[\nabla_u,C].
}
\]

On spectral slots \(x\to y\), this becomes the divided-difference law

\[
[\nabla_u,f(C)]_{xy}
=f^{[1]}(x,y)E_{u,xy}.
\]

The analogous Poisson-side identity is

\[
\boxed{
[f(C),\mathcal J_u]_{xy}
=f^{[1]}(x,y)[C,\mathcal J_u]_{xy}.
}
\]

This is the structural reason helicity, shifted spectral cuts, radial/modulus readers, and related work functionals repeatedly factor through common latent amplitudes.

For the complete state-encoding theorem of the mother/spectral-flag family, see the separate [spectral-signature core](../spectral_signature/README.md).

## 3. Euler–heat holonomy

Let

\[
E(u)=B(u,u),
\qquad
H(u)=Lu,
\qquad
L=-C^2.
\]

For the state-space Lie bracket convention

\[
\bigl[F,G\bigr](u)=DG(u)F(u)-DF(u)G(u),
\]

define the bilinear heat defect

\[
\boxed{
Q_L(a,b)
=L B(a,b)-B(La,b)-B(a,Lb).
}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\bigl[E,H\bigr](u)=Q_L(u,u).}
\]

For ordinary Laplacian heat this is the polarized carré-du-champ of the Euler product.  The next mixed levels are still generated by \(B\), \(L\), and \(Q_L\):

\[
\boxed{
[E,[E,H]]
=2Q_L(u,B(u,u))-2B(u,Q_L(u,u)),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
[H,[E,H]]
=2Q_L(u,Lu)-LQ_L(u,u).
}
\]

Jacobi locks apparently different descendant routes.  For example, if \(A=[E,H]\),

\[
\boxed{
[E,[H,A]]=[H,[E,A]].
}
\]

The research audits evaluated independent mixed Lie words through length six and observed the free-Lie dimensions

\[
2,1,2,3,6,9.
\]

This is recorded as structural evidence: dynamic complexity grows through bracket words of the two primitive flows, not through discovery of additional primitive dynamical terms.

## 4. Pressure and spectral interference

Modewise, Hodge reconstruction is isometric:

\[
|\widehat{\nabla u}(k)|^2=|\widehat\omega(k)|^2.
\]

Hence the pressure source

\[
g=|\nabla u|^2-|\omega|^2
\]

has no one-mode contribution.  For Fourier modes \(k,\ell\), the pair contribution can be written

\[
A_k:A_\ell-\omega_k\cdot\omega_\ell
=\operatorname{tr}(A_kA_\ell),
\qquad A_k=i\hat u(k)\otimes k,
\]

and vanishes for collinear incompressible pairs.  Thus pressure source is a cross-mode/interference object, which explains why modal stocks alone can lose dynamically relevant phase information.

## 5. Obstruction status

The formation datum generates the equation.  A putative deeper obstruction is therefore not identified with a raw amplitude such as \([\nabla_u,C]\) or helicity torsion: those can be nonzero on harmless invariant classes.

The current structural target is instead a curvature/cohomology class of the action of the Euler–heat descendant algebra on the Hodge/curl representations.  A canonical quotient defining that class is still open; this directory does not claim such a cohomology theorem has been completed.
